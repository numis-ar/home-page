There are bookshops that sell books, and there are bookshops that sell books in their own cooperative's currency. **Babel** is one of the latter: a book market with a currency of its own, run by Numis, where some forty titles —mostly Argentine literature, from Borges to Mariana Enríquez— are sold in numis and paid for with [GNU Taler](https://taler.net/es/).

## Real numis, not a points system

Numis are not points you trade for discounts: they are the cooperative's currency. Every contribution you make to Numis is received in numis, ready to spend in the market. The price you see on the page is the final price; there is no hidden financial cost folded into every book.

## Paying with Taler

When you check out, Babel shows a QR code. You scan it with your GNU Taler wallet —there are free, open-source apps for iPhone, Android, and Chrome— and the payment goes straight from the wallet. No card is involved, no installments, no interest: it's prepaid, so you can't overspend.

And privacy is not a promise — it's the design: the wallet tells the shop nothing about you. The cooperative gets paid and can audit its income; what you bought and when you bought it is known only to you.

## No accounts, no emails

Babel asks for no email, no username, no password. Your order is found again by its own address, which the wallet keeps along with the payment. That also means the order link is the key to the order: whoever has it, sees it.

> Today there is no way to recover it remotely, and we say so plainly.

That's how the help page itself puts it. Losing the link is not the end of the world —with your name and phone number we'll look it up at any of our locations—, but an honest answer matters more than a comfortable one. With no account and no email there is no password to forget, no data to guard, no spam.

## Shipping, with no fine print

Shipping is charged separately and is calculated at checkout, based on your postal code and the weight of the package, and it is shown before you pay. If you pick it up yourself, it costs nothing. Orders are prepared and dispatched through Correo Argentino, and you track the status on the order page itself: when it ships, the tracking number appears; when it's ready for pickup, the status says so.

## A real place

Behind the website there are real bookshops. You can pick up orders and buy at Babel Chacarita (Jorge Newbery 3550), Babel Once (Av. Rivadavia 2345), or the Warnes 1200 warehouse. The help page is the only remote contact channel —there is no email address, no call center—, and if something isn't explained there, the shop is a place you can actually go to.

Today Babel is running in a testing environment, one step before opening the door to the whole cooperative. Trying it end to end —picking a book, calculating shipping, reaching the payment code— is already possible. The only thing missing to close the loop is paying with a wallet loaded with numis, and that will happen too. It's the same path we will walk in November together with the Universidad Nacional de Quilmes at the fair: what today is tested with books, tomorrow is sustained by the cooperative's economy.
